I am a 5th year Ph.D. student at the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, CSE Department, advised by Pratik Jawanpuria and Ganesh Ramakrishnan, working on large-scale efficient machine learning, generously supported by the Prime Minister Research Fellowship (2022–2027) and the Microsoft PhD Award (2025–2026).

I have been a Student Researcher (twice) at Google DeepMind India, hosted by Pradeep Shenoy during (July 2023–Nov 2023 & Dec 2023–May 2024), working on Efficient Pretraining of Large Language Models and Knowledge Distillation for Large Language Models for proprietary Gemini and LaMDA models. During my time at DeepMind, I had the pleasure of working with and being mentored by Dheeraj Nagaraj, Arun Suggala and Prateek Jain.

Recently, I also interned at Microsoft Research India, where I worked on generalizable and efficient test-time scaling with Nagarajan Natarajan, Amit Sharma and Subbarao Kambhampati (see our work on Interwhen).

My research develops optimization and statistical methods for improving learning under data, computation, and deployment constraints. I study three decision points in the learning pipeline: what data to learn from, how models should adapt, and how to evaluate or constrain their outputs. Across these settings, I seek formulations that make task structure explicit and expose measurable trade-offs, with formal guarantees where possible:
  • Structured data selection. I formulate subset selection as structured optimization rather than relying on fixed importance scores. Examples include bandit-guided submodular curriculum selection, validation-guided gradient matching under partition-matroid budgets, and uniform prototype selection via partial optimal transport and submodular optimization. I extend this perspective to graph-structured allocation of language-model training data and coreset-based personalization in federated learning.
  • Continual learning and distribution shift. Learning over time requires balancing plasticity against retention and robustness. In continual learning, Sculpting Subspaces constrains full fine-tuning updates to reduce interference with previously learned tasks. For changing data, online distributional prediction under drift and corruption studies how latent cluster structure can support robust prediction. In federated learning, Bayesian client-specific coresets address personalization by selecting representative data for each client rather than training on each client's full dataset.
  • Reliable and verifiable model behavior. I also study how to align model behavior with task-level requirements. Interwhen uses test-time verification to steer reasoning models; ClinicR develops reasoning-based prompting for open-ended medical question answering; and FairPO addresses fairness in multi-label preference optimization. Together, these works treat reliability, not only predictive accuracy, as an explicit design objective.
Across these lines, my goal is to develop learning algorithms whose use of data, updates, and computation is explicit and principled: improving sample efficiency while maintaining coverage, enabling adaptation while controlling interference, and making model outputs more verifiable and aligned with the task. Before my Ph.D., I was a Research Software Engineer (SCAI Fellow) at Microsoft Research India Bangalore, hosted by Amit Sharma and Vageesh Chandramouli. Before that, I completed my undergrad at IIEST Shibpur, advised by Prof. Susanta Chakrabarty (former Dean) and Prof. Asit Kr. Das.

Fun Fact: During my undergrad, I was an astrophysics enthusiast and often contemplated career choices. I spent much of that time contributing to SunPy (a Solar Physics Python library) alongside researchers at NASA GSFC, and in 2024 got recognized with the NASA Group Achievement Award.
